01Buttons

Preserve the content. Resolve the surrounding space.

Control slots stay 6u square. Chip slots stay 4u. Border occupies its owner’s interior once, including when transparent.

02Button geometry

Five arrangements · three densities · Sharpest and Pill

Compare label, icon, glyph, avatar and logo arrangements. Label edges depend on exposed content, not density or curvature. Shape pairs retain the same dimensions.

Scroll horizontally or focus the comparison and use arrow keys.

03Listbox geometry

Icon, avatar and logo rows

Each listbox is an explicit composite. Requested inset sets its gap; child spacing resolves without shrinking text or slots.

04Menus & listboxes

Actions, selections and composed inputs.

Use the arrow keys to navigate listboxes and Enter to select. Input adornments use chips inside neutral wrappers; their shape is independent of attached-composite rules.

05Tables & data-lists

Alignment makes relationships legible.

Collection density controls row spacing. Multiline copy grows the row while preserving slots and the density of nested actions.

06Containers

Ordinary cards preserve their children’s geometry.

Card inset and gap use 3u / 4.5u / 6u. Card density never applies composite subtraction to a nested control.

One card · independent families

The card, collection and controls each resolve their own density. Change one family in the inspector to compare.

07Spacing families

Compact / Default / Loose

Controls use inset 0.5u / 1u / 2u; collections use 1u / 2u / 3u; cards use 3u / 4.5u / 6u. These are independent lookups, not a cascading multiplier.

08Composable curvature

Shape follows the owning component.

Soft is 3u. Softest controls are capsules; Softest containers use 4u. Attached composites consume the effective parent inset once and preserve their child slots.
