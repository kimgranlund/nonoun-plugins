# Page narrative and module contribution

Use this guidance for marketing and narrative content experiences. For mixed websites and products, apply it to the relevant surface rather than imposing a storytelling sequence on every task. The machine-readable prompts live in the [pattern registry](patterns/registry.json), under `reasoningGuidance`.

## Page reasoning

- **Purpose:** What progress should the visitor make, and what outcome should the organization achieve?
- **Audience context:** What brings the visitor here? What do they already understand, doubt, need or expect?
- **Story:** What central idea and supporting evidence could move them toward that outcome?
- **Expression:** How is that story best experienced: explanation, demonstration, comparison, exploration, testimony or a combination?
- **Decomposition:** What meaningful, digestible parts does the story need? Each part should resolve a question, establish something or enable a decision.
- **Scope and emphasis:** How much attention, detail and space does each part deserve?
- **Sequence and rhythm:** What should precede what? Where should the experience accelerate, pause, deepen or change form?
- **Invitation:** What next action is appropriate at each point, given the visitor’s understanding, confidence and readiness?

## Module reasoning

- **Contribution:** What specific job does this module perform within the larger experience?
- **Content and evidence:** What must it communicate or demonstrate to perform that job?
- **Representation:** Which pattern makes that content easiest to understand, assess or act upon?
- **Scale:** What prominence, density, depth and interaction does its role justify?
- **Relationship:** How does it connect to adjacent modules without unnecessary repetition?
- **Resolution:** What should the visitor understand, believe or be able to do afterward?

## How to use the two levels

Use these as revisitable prompts, not a required sequence, questionnaire or scoring rubric. Scale the depth to the decision.

Reason from visitor progress and available means together. Missing evidence can change the story; a strong demonstration can replace several explanations.

Treat spatial footprint, visual emphasis, information density, reading effort, disclosure depth and interaction demand as independent scale decisions. A short statement can carry more narrative weight than a large table.

Choose a module for its contribution and content relationship, then evaluate it in the page sequence. Variety earns its place through comprehension, attention or decision support.

Record concise design conclusions, assumptions and tradeoffs in the brief and relevant intent/rationale fields. Do not require a transcript of private model reasoning or new reviewer form fields.

Evaluate comprehension, qualified progression and confident decisions with explicit definitions and guardrails. Clicks are one signal; prototype preference is not evidence of business impact.

Page decisions establish responsibilities and relationships for modules. Module decisions can expose a gap, excessive scope or a better way to tell the story, prompting a page revision. Neither level is a one-way handoff. Preserve coherent terminology, spacing and interaction while varying the representation when its job changes.

For example, a prospective customer may need to understand how a handoff works before considering a pilot. A working specimen might resolve that question more directly than several feature cards. A comparison matrix would be appropriate only if the next question concerns comparable alternatives and the necessary evidence exists. The invitation follows readiness; it does not need to follow every module.
