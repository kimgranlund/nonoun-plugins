# Platform websites

## Intent and boundary

Help people understand an ecosystem, locate their role within it, evaluate dependencies, and find a credible adoption or participation path. Here “platform website” means the public ecosystem and adoption experience. Use [product UI](product.md) for the authenticated console or workspace. A single project may need both profiles with an explicit transition.

| Surface family | User intent and content responsibilities | Candidate next step | Candidate success signal |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Platform overview | Understand the system's capabilities, boundaries and relationships | Follow a route appropriate to role or goal | Accurate mental model and successful routing |
| Solutions / architecture | Understand how capabilities combine to solve a problem | Evaluate architecture or requirements | Feasibility assessment and identified dependencies |
| Integrations / connectors | Verify compatibility, prerequisites, ownership and support | Inspect setup, test or connect | Successful eligible setup; fewer unsupported attempts |
| Marketplace / ecosystem catalog | Compare offerings by relevance, trust and compatibility | Inspect, install or contact a provider | Suitable discovery and successful adoption |
| Developer entry | Determine what can be built and how to reach a first working result | Follow quickstart or inspect reference material | Time to verified first result, including recovery |
| Partner / contributor | Understand participation criteria, obligations and benefits | Apply, contribute or request clarification | Qualified participation and completed onboarding |
| Trust / governance | Evaluate reliability, data boundaries, ownership and commitments | Verify applicable documentation or contact an owner | Resolution of adoption blockers without unsupported promises |
| Adoption / migration | Understand prerequisites, sequencing, risk and support | Plan, pilot or migrate | Successful staged adoption and recovery from incompatibility |

## Content strategy

Use a shared vocabulary for capabilities and relationships, then provide role-specific depth. A developer needs executable prerequisites; a buyer may need scope and responsibilities; a partner may need participation rules. Do not require every visitor to understand the entire architecture before progressing. Explain transitions between public content, documentation, marketplace, account creation and signed-in tools.

## Decision space

Explore role-first entry when participants have distinct goals, capability-first exploration when visitors know what they need, or outcome-first adoption paths when the ecosystem is unfamiliar. A catalog might prioritize guided compatibility, open exploration or curated starting points. Avoid depicting everything as a product card if relationships and dependencies are the core decision.

## Measures and guardrails

Candidate measures include route success by role, compatible integration adoption, verified first result, qualified partner onboarding and unresolved adoption questions. Pair install/signup counts with working activation and support burden. Define what “working” means; reaching a docs page is not a successful integration. Be explicit about attribution when completion occurs on another service.

## Review questions

Can each participant find the right entry path? Are prerequisites and responsibilities visible before commitment? Can someone distinguish a first-party feature from an external integration? Does the next surface preserve vocabulary, identity and task context?
