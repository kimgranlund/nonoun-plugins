# Agentic workspaces

## Intent

Help people delegate useful work, retain appropriate authority, understand progress and intervene when needed. Start with the goal, available tools and evidence, operating scope, consequences and uncertainty. A chat box, confidence number or activity feed is not by itself a supervision model.

## Candidate responsibilities

- Direction: establish outcome, constraints, sources, budget and stopping conditions.
- Planning: assess proposed work, dependencies, alternatives and unresolved assumptions.
- Choices: compare options against explicit criteria, including deferral or requesting another proposal.
- Authorization: understand the exact action, scope, changes, consequences and expiry of approval.
- Supervision: identify exceptions, blocked work, escalation and changed assumptions.
- Evidence: inspect source provenance, freshness, limitations and concise rationale.
- Results: verify deliverables, unresolved issues, downstream effects and continuation or rollback where available.
- Durable instructions: distinguish task-specific directions from persistent preferences or policy.

These responsibilities can share a surface or span a flow. Choose by oversight needs, frequency and action consequence, not by a mandatory console inventory.

## Content and alternatives

Distinguish observation, inference, recommendation, policy, authority, execution and outcome. A model probability or confidence score is not an authorization or a guarantee of correctness. Explain what a score describes and its evidence limits; use actual calibration evidence before claiming reliability. Activity logs should show observable events and concise explanations, not imply access to private reasoning.

Alternative hypotheses might center on an exception inbox, a mission workspace or a scheduled briefing. Compare what each makes visible, what it asks a person to supervise and what can be missed. Sources, action scope and approval semantics should remain consistent across alternatives.

## Evaluation and boundaries

Consider successful outcomes, appropriate interventions, correction effort and authority compliance alongside missed escalation, unwanted actions and automation surprise. Separate design-review choices from simulated product approvals. Static wireframes cannot validate agent reliability or real execution permissions.
