# Marketing websites

## Intent

Help a prospective customer determine relevance, understand value and limitations, trust credible evidence, and choose an appropriate next step. Organizational goals may include qualified demand, adoption or sales efficiency. Audience understanding and fit are guardrails against maximizing clicks indiscriminately.

The inventory below is a set of candidate responsibilities, not a default sitemap or section order. A small offering may combine several responsibilities in one page; a complex buying process may require separate journeys for evaluator, user and approver.

| Surface family | User intent and content responsibilities | Candidate next step | Candidate success signal |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Homepage | Orient an unfamiliar visitor; establish relevance, differentiated outcome and routes by need | Explore an offering or relevant use case | Correct understanding of offering and successful routing |
| Product / feature | Determine fit; explain capabilities, examples, limitations and prerequisites | Try, evaluate or request a relevant demonstration | Qualified evaluation and expectation accuracy |
| Use case / industry | Recognize a situation; connect the problem, workflow, outcome and evidence | Explore a matching solution | Recognition of fit and useful progression |
| Pricing / packaging | Compare entitlements, costs, limits and commitments | Select a suitable plan or clarify a constraint | Appropriate plan selection; low surprise or correction |
| Comparison / alternatives | Understand meaningful differences fairly, including unsuitable cases | Choose an evaluation path | Decision confidence and accurate differentiation |
| Customer story / proof | Examine credibility, context, method and transferability of an outcome | Assess applicability or inspect supporting detail | Correct interpretation of evidence and its limits |
| About / trust | Understand who is responsible and why the organization is credible | Verify qualifications, contact or continue evaluation | Resolution of consequential trust questions |
| Campaign landing | Fulfil the promise of an acquisition message with focused evidence | Complete the campaign's legitimate next step | Message match and qualified completion |
| Contact / demo | Explain what information is needed and what happens after submission | Submit, schedule or use an alternative contact route | Successful handoff and appropriate expectations |

## Content strategy

Match claims to available evidence and the visitor's stage of understanding. Distinguish product facts, proof, examples and aspirations. Address the objections that change a decision; do not insert every possible objection. Clarify whether the next step is self-service, a trial, a purchase or a human conversation. Supplied brand voice shapes expression, not factual entitlement to make a claim.

## Decision space

Possible hypotheses include outcome-led explanation for visitors who recognize the problem, capability-led exploration for informed evaluators, proof-led evaluation when credibility is uncertain, or a guided route when visitors struggle to identify fit. These are examples. Choose alternatives based on the project's largest uncertainty, not a fixed hero-template list.

## Measures and guardrails

Define qualified next-step completion by audience and acquisition source; pair it with comprehension, poor-fit activation, support demand or expectation accuracy. For multi-stakeholder purchases, a useful return visit or shared evaluation material may matter more than immediate signup. Do not equate page engagement with persuasion or invent conversion benchmarks.

## Review questions

Can a visitor explain whom this is for, the useful outcome, relevant limitations and the next step? Is the evidence specific enough to assess? Can someone recognize non-fit without pressure? Is the same claim consistent across product, pricing and campaign surfaces?

## Page and module reasoning

Use [page narrative and module contribution](../narrative-reasoning.md) to connect purpose, audience, story, expression, decomposition, scope, rhythm and invitation. Revisit page decisions as module choices expose evidence gaps or better representations. These are reasoning prompts, not a mandatory section sequence.

## Header responsibilities

Choose [header composition](../patterns/navigation.md#marketing-header-composition) separately from destination hierarchy. Identity, primary routes and utilities/actions form explicit groups. Evaluate brand-led, minimal, centered or enterprise composition against discovery needs and readiness for action. Keep brand/navigation/action centers aligned, preserve access when compacting, and avoid adding unsupported search or account features. Header consistency supports orientation even where page content varies its rhythm.
