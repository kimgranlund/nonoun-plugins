# Content websites

## Intent

Help people discover, assess, understand and revisit information. A publication, knowledge resource and specialist archive may share content surfaces but have different definitions of value. Establish whether the audience seeks an answer, follows a subject, studies a sequence or explores without a fixed query.

| Surface family | User intent and content responsibilities | Candidate next step | Candidate success signal |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Publication / resource home | Understand subject scope, editorial priorities and freshness | Read, search or follow a topic | Relevant discovery rather than undirected clicks |
| Topic hub | Build a mental map of a subject and its subtopics | Explore a meaningful branch | Improved orientation and useful onward selection |
| Search / results | Locate information and assess relevance before opening | Inspect a result or refine | Answer-finding and result relevance |
| Article / explanation | Understand a claim, argument or procedure with context and evidence | Apply, verify, save or continue | Comprehension, answer confidence and successful application |
| Reference / documentation | Retrieve precise information, prerequisites and version context | Use or verify the information | Correct task execution and fewer version mismatches |
| Collection / series | Understand a relationship or progression among pieces | Start, resume or skip appropriately | Progress with understanding, not forced completion |
| Author / source | Assess expertise, provenance and perspective | Inspect related work or evidence | Informed credibility judgment |
| Subscription / newsletter | Understand subject, cadence, value and control over delivery | Subscribe or adjust preferences | Relevant retained readership and easy withdrawal |
| Saved / history | Return to something useful with context intact | Resume or organize | Successful retrieval and resumption |

## Content strategy

Model content objects and their relationships: subject, author, source, date, version, difficulty, format and intended audience where relevant. Metadata should answer real selection questions. Distinguish publication date, modification date and underlying event date. Plan for outdated, corrected, superseded and unavailable material. Recommendations should have an intelligible relationship to the current need.

## Decision space

Compare query-first retrieval, topic-led exploration and editorially guided discovery when those reflect actual entry intents. Reading alternatives may prioritize uninterrupted explanation, progressive depth, or reference-oriented scanning. Do not force an immersive reading design onto a quick reference task or treat a feed as the only modern discovery model.

## Measures and guardrails

Candidate measures include answer success, comprehension, return retrieval and relevant subscription retention. A short visit may mean efficient success; a long visit may mean either engagement or confusion. Pair recirculation with relevance and cognitive burden. Separate commercial objectives from editorial integrity and avoid evaluating content quality solely through traffic.

## Review questions

Can a reader assess relevance, provenance, currency and expected effort? Can they resume without retracing the whole site? Does related content deepen or distract from the current intent? Are content relationships maintained when the catalog grows?
