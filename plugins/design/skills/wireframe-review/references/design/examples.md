# Worked intent briefs

These fictional examples illustrate reasoning structure. Their outcomes, constraints and measures are not claims about real products. The alternative lists are examples rather than a mandatory trio.

## Marketing: evaluating a specialist product

**Profiles:** Marketing; optional commerce if purchase belongs in scope.

**Intent:** An evaluator needs to know whether the product fits an existing workflow before investing in a trial. The organization wants suitable prospects to begin evaluation.

**Content responsibilities:** Explain the outcome, show a representative input/output, identify compatibility and important limits, and explain what evaluation requires. Actual claims and proof must come from the project; fictional evidence is unacceptable.

**Primary action:** Begin a suitable evaluation. Legitimate alternatives include investigating compatibility, sharing evidence with an approver, or deciding against the product.

**KPI candidate:** Qualified evaluation completion among eligible visitors in a defined cohort/window. Qualification, baseline and target are unknown until supplied. Early review can test whether people explain fit and prerequisites accurately. Guardrails include poor-fit trials and misleading expectations.

**Hypotheses:** A outcome-led narrative may help visitors who recognize the problem; B an inspectable example may help those unsure about quality; C a compatibility-led route may help evaluators with hard constraints. Keep the same verified facts and available next steps across proposals. Compare understanding and unresolved uncertainty, not preferred hero appearance.

**Scope:** Product page and its relevant next-step states. No invented requirement for a pricing page, testimonial strip or campaign funnel.

## Mobile: resuming interrupted field work

**Profiles:** Mobile + product + operations + lifecycle.

**Intent:** A worker must resume a partially completed inspection after losing connectivity, knowing which changes are local and which are confirmed.

**Content responsibilities:** Identify the job and location, saved progress, remaining required input, sync state and conflicts. Explain how to continue safely when a prerequisite cannot be verified.

**Primary action:** Complete the inspection accurately or defer it with a clear reason. An unverified local save must not be presented as a server-confirmed submission.

**KPI candidate:** Valid resumed inspections completed among eligible interrupted inspections; define the interruption window and completion event with the project. Evaluate preservation of entered work and recognition of sync state in review. Guardrails include duplicate submission, lost data and invalid completion.

**Hypotheses:** A a guided resumption sequence may reduce uncertainty; B a section overview may help experienced workers jump to incomplete items; C a contextual “next unresolved item” surface may reduce navigation while retaining access to the whole job. Compare accuracy, effort and recoverability, including permission or connectivity failure.

**Scope:** Render interrupted, resumed, conflict and completed states. The HTML shell can link between them. Offline storage, device capture and synchronization remain described behavior until implemented.

## Dashboard: triaging a service anomaly

**Profiles:** Dashboard + operations + collaboration.

**Intent:** An operator needs to decide whether a change is meaningful, identify its scope, and hand it to the right owner with evidence.

**Content responsibilities:** State the metric, time window, baseline, coverage/freshness, affected entities and confidence limits. Preserve filters into drill-down and distinguish acknowledgement from resolution.

**Primary action:** Investigate or route a supported issue; choosing no intervention can be correct when evidence does not justify one.

**KPI candidate:** Correct triage among assessed candidate anomalies, with the classification method and observation window agreed in the project. Measure explanation accuracy in a prototype; do not claim reduced incident duration from a static dashboard. Guardrails include false alarms, missed anomalies and stale-data decisions.

**Hypotheses:** A exception-first triage may help time-constrained monitoring; B a metric-to-driver path may help diagnosis; C a narrative summary with traceable evidence may help cross-role handoffs. Use the same dataset, uncertainty and task prompts across alternatives.

**Scope:** Overview, evidence drill-down and handoff states. Sample data must be labeled. Live alerts and real causal diagnosis are not simulated by static cards.

## Administration: granting access

**Profiles:** Product + operations.

**Intent:** An authorized administrator must grant the minimum appropriate access to a collaborator and verify the resulting scope.

**Content responsibilities:** Identify the person, workspace, role meaning, inherited access, consequences and revocation path. Source the permission model from the project rather than inventing one.

**KPI candidate:** Correct permission assignment on the first confirmed attempt, with rework and unintended access as guardrails. Faster assignment is useful only when correctness is preserved.

**Hypotheses:** A person-first assignment, B resource-first sharing and C a guided role comparison may serve different administrative mental models. Evaluate whether the administrator predicts the resulting access and recognizes exceptions. Do not vary access enforcement or omit consequential confirmation to make a proposal appear faster.

## Reasoning checks when maintaining the corpus

Use these prompts as exercises when changing guidance; they are not automated proof of design quality:

- A five-page specialist publication should produce discovery and reading responsibilities, not a mandatory conversion funnel.
- A mobile analytics app should combine mobile continuity with analytical questions, rather than choosing exactly one profile.
- A partner platform's public entry and its admin console should have distinct user intents and transitions.
- An AI editor should explain correction, context and action boundaries, not automatically become a chat screen.
- A checkout brief without pricing or policies should mark those facts missing instead of inventing them.
- A repeat-use operator tool should expose error/recovery and bulk scope, not add first-use onboarding to every session.

For each exercise, examine the selected references, intent, content responsibilities, candidate measures, guardrails, alternative hypotheses and prototype limits. A response that repeats page names but cannot explain the user's decision has not used the corpus adequately.
