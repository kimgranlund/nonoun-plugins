# Recipe: words, a name, a voice or a visual direction

Read before step 3 of the procedure when the question is how something should sound, read, be called or look.

| | |
|---|---|
| Specimen | The same piece made in each direction: the paragraph written, the name in use on three surfaces, the screen or mark drawn. Never a description of a tone in place of the tone. |
| Fixture | One brief and one set of pieces, the same for every variant, including the awkward ones (the error message, the legal line, the very short label, the long explanation). |
| Stress | Named cases as a button group marked `data-fb-control-group`: short form and long form, another medium, a cold reader who knows nothing, the piece beside its competitors. |

## Choosing variants

The usual axes: who is speaking, how close they stand to the reader, plain or figurative, what is left out, what
the piece borrows from (a manual, a letter, a poster). Two variants that differ by adjectives are one variant.

## Rules for the specimens

- Every variant makes every piece of the fixture. A direction shown only on the piece that flatters it is not
  shown.
- Name a variant by what it does ("Says the cost first"), not by a mood word.
- A name is shown in use: said in a sentence, shortened, as an address, next to the names it will sit among.
- A visual direction is drawn with the page's own stand-in values unless it stands for a system; then it takes
  that system's values (see `ui.md`).
- Aspects are what a reader can feel and say: "Sounds like us", "Clear on first read", "Holds up when short".
